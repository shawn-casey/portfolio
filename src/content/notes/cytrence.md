## Product & Growth, August 2026 to present

Remote

I own go-to-market content strategy for a hardware device launch, and I lead use-case development across the **industrial** and **datacenter** segments.

The work is figuring out who the device is actually for and proving it in specifics. An industrial buyer and a datacenter buyer want the same box for different reasons, and the pitch that lands with one is noise to the other. Use-case development means going and finding the concrete workflow each of them is stuck on, then making sure the product story starts there rather than at the feature list.

It's the other half of the [[Marcel]] problem, from the selling side instead of the building side. Both come down to finding a specific person with a specific expensive problem before writing a line of anything.

## Firmware Engineering Intern, summer 2024

Burlington, MA · paid, formal internship

![|inset](/img/cytrence.webp)

Embedded firmware demos, the builds that go in front of people who need to see the thing work on hardware, in a room, without a safety net. And real-time latency profiling tools to measure where time actually went, because on embedded work "it feels laggy" isn't a bug report; you need numbers per stage before you can argue about which stage to fix.

Some of that shipped as open source: [[Kiwi-Pico Firmware]].

![Robotics Summit & Expo floor.|wide](/img/robotics-summit.webp)

I presented technical demos at the **Boston Robotics Summit** to investors and developers. Doing both at once is its own skill: investors want the outcome and the market, developers want to know what's happening under the hood and will spot handwaving instantly. Same demo, both audiences, neither patronized.

It was also my first real exposure to the bottom of the stack. Clock cycles, interrupt latency, hardware that doesn't care about your abstractions. Everything I've done since in mobile and [[Belcan|cyber-physical]] work sits on top of it.
