**M.S. Cybersecurity, Cyber-Physical Systems specialization**
Atlanta, GA · expected December 2027

The track is about systems whose failures are physical: control systems, embedded devices, medical devices, vehicles, critical infrastructure. I picked it because it's the through-line of what I'd already done. [[Belcan]] was a jet engine's telemetry. [[Cytrence Technologies]] was firmware and interrupt latency. [[Marcel]] reads a CNC control's screen. [[Visa]] was mobile devices treated as hardware to pry open.

> [!info] The consequences don't stop at the data.
> In IT security the worst case is information: stolen, altered, unavailable. In CPS the worst case is a machine *doing something*. A valve opens. A motor spins up. A control loop accepts a bad sensor reading and acts on it. You can't roll that back from a snapshot.

Which cuts both ways, and the second way is more interesting: the physics is also a defense. Real systems obey conservation laws and have bounded dynamics, so a physically impossible data stream is detectable no matter how well-formed the packets were. That's an invariant an attacker can't forge, and it doesn't exist in pure-IT settings.

Atlanta also puts me next to a concentration of payments and critical-infrastructure security work, which was not an accident.
