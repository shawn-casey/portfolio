IMTS 2026, McCormick Place, Chicago, September 14 to 19.

I went with one question: does reading the control screen actually work on the machines shops are buying now? [[Marcel]] depends on it, and until this month my answer was a guess.

## What I had assumed

That keystroke input was largely infeasible. FANUC MDI panels are proprietary, so a device that captures the display and injects keyboard input would be fighting the control at both ends.

That's true of older iron. It is not true of the current controls I got to look at.

## What I saw

The controls I looked at, current FANUC controls and OnLogic-based industrial PCs, shared three properties:

1. **Windows-based HMI.** The operator interface runs on a standard PC layer, not a closed panel.
2. **USB keyboard and mouse.** Standard input, which is what a KVM-style capture device speaks.
3. **Standard display connection.** An interchangeable monitor on a common video interface, so a capture device sits inline without modification.

Put those together and the display bridge needs **no network drop, no licensed protocol, and no builder integration.** It sits between the control and its own monitor and reads what the operator reads.

## What this is and isn't

This is a handful of machines at one trade show. It's what I observed, not a survey of the installed base, and the shop floor has a lot of older iron on it that this says nothing about. The right register is: on the current controls I could get in front of, the screen is a common interface, and that is enough to build against.

The part I'd repeat to anyone is smaller than the finding. I had a belief about what was feasible, it was based on the wrong generation of hardware, and the fix was to go stand in front of the machines. That's cheaper than most of the ways I could have found out.
