A summer detailing at a **Honda dealership** taught me the trade properly. Production work: a fixed number of cars a day, a quality bar somebody else set, no room to be slow. Paint correction, interior extraction, what removes what, and how long each honestly takes.

Then I went back to school and realized every student on campus had a filthy car and no time. Bought my own equipment and started detailing for students. Mobile, meaning I came to them, which is the whole product. The competition was a car wash across town that costs you an afternoon. I cost you a parking spot and your keys.

Bringing on a **partner** changed its shape. More cars per weekend, but the bigger effect was word of mouth: a second social circle roughly doubled the top of the funnel.

## The HuskyTHON partnership

I partnered with **[[University of Connecticut|HuskyTHON]]** and donated half of all earnings to Connecticut Children's Hospital.

> [!success] $3,000 raised personally through the partnership.

I expected it to cost me money. It grew the business instead. A detail that funds a children's hospital is an easy yes and an easy thing to tell your roommate about, and it reached people who were never going to pay a stranger to clean their car on the merits. Customers who came in that way stayed.

What grows a small business is usually a reason to talk about it, not a better product. And the good version and the profitable version overlap more often than people assume, if you're willing to run the math on half margins.

> [!info] Two numbers, kept straight
> The $3,000 is what I personally raised here. HuskyTHON as an organization raised **$2.1M** for the same hospital, which is the org total from my [[University of Connecticut|DEI Captain]] work, a separate role at the same event.
